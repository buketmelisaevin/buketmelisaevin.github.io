HUMAN-AI UX • PRODUCT DESIGN • UX EVALUATION

Designing a clearer conversation with AI.

A conceptual redesign exploring how ChatGPT could better support prompting, comprehension and transparency without making the interaction more complicated.

The Context

Powerful systems still depend on clear interaction.

Conversational AI removes many of the visible controls found in traditional interfaces. Instead, users communicate what they want through language.

That flexibility is powerful, but it also moves more responsibility onto the user: they need to know what information to provide, how to phrase a useful request and how to interpret the response they receive.

I explored how the interface itself could take a more active role in supporting that process.

Evaluation Approach

Looking beyond the chat box.

Rather than treating ChatGPT as a conventional interface, I examined the relationship between the user, the AI system and the information exchanged between them.

01

Human-AI interaction

I considered where responsibility sits between the person and the system: communicating intent, asking for missing information, interpreting output and deciding what to trust.

02

Cognitive effort

I examined where users are expected to hold too much information in memory or process large amounts of output at once.

03

Understandable AI

The concept drew on explainable and understandable-AI interaction patterns, including ideas explored through Emily Campbell's Shape of AI framework.

i
Project scope

This was a conceptual Human-AI UX redesign, not a usability study. The proposed solutions therefore represent design hypotheses that would require user testing and further iteration before implementation.

Core UX Problems

Three moments where the interface could provide more support.

01

Prompting depends on user expertise

Users are given an almost completely open input field, but the quality of the result can depend heavily on whether they know which context, constraints and output requirements to provide.

Interaction burden begins before the AI responds.
02

Useful answers can become difficult to scan

Long responses place large amounts of information into one continuous vertical stream, making it harder to locate, prioritise and return to individual pieces of information.

More information does not always mean more clarity.
03

Credibility requires additional effort

When users want to understand where information came from or assess supporting material, that evidence can feel separate from the main reading experience.

Trust should be supported by the interface, not assumed.

Design Challenge

How can an AI interface help users communicate intent, understand responses and judge information without making the conversation feel more complicated?

Design Principles

Add support without taking away flexibility.

01

Guide, don't prescribe

Help users construct stronger requests without turning conversation into a rigid form.

02

Reveal complexity progressively

Keep the default interaction approachable while making additional structure available when it becomes useful.

03

Support understanding

Structure information around how people scan, compare and revisit content.

04

Preserve familiarity

Improve the interaction without replacing the conversational patterns users already understand.

Intervention 01

PromptGPT

Instead of expecting users to understand prompt engineering, PromptGPT acts as a temporary layer of support when a request would benefit from more structure.

The aim is not to write the request for the user. It is to help them identify the information the AI needs.

PromptGPT is deliberately temporary. It holds the prompt-building context only for the current request, presents the structured prompt for review and editing, and disappears once the prompt is submitted so it does not permanently occupy the conversation.

Intervention 02

Make long answers easier to navigate.

The redesign introduces clearer structure and progressive disclosure so users can understand the shape of a response before reading every detail.

01

Scan first

Stronger hierarchy makes the main sections visible immediately.

02

Expand when needed

Supporting detail can remain available without dominating the initial response.

03

Return to information

Distinct sections create stronger landmarks within longer conversations.

Intervention 03

Make supporting information easier to inspect.

Transparency is more useful when evidence is connected to the content the user is already reading rather than feeling like a separate research task.

01

Evidence stays connected

Supporting material is presented as part of the answer experience rather than an afterthought.

02

Optional, not intrusive

Users who simply want the answer are not forced through additional information.

03

Supports informed trust

Users are given clearer opportunities to inspect the basis of information rather than relying on presentation alone.

04

Resource updates stay visible

The “New resources added” status makes newly available supporting material visible without interrupting the conversation.

05

A predictable place to return

The resource-library shortcut provides a persistent entry point for revisiting supporting material from the conversation.

Final Concept

Familiar conversation.
More support around it.

The concept deliberately keeps the core chat interaction recognisable while introducing support at the moments where users need to communicate, navigate or evaluate information.

Reflection

Designing AI means designing the relationship, not only the screen.

This project shifted my focus from treating AI as a feature inside an interface to considering the complete interaction between the person and the system.

A conversational interface may appear simple, but users are still making complex decisions: how to communicate intent, what information to provide, how much of a response to process and whether the output should be trusted.

The strongest opportunity was therefore not to add more controls everywhere, but to introduce support only when it reduces effort or improves understanding.

The next step would be to test these design hypotheses with users, particularly whether PromptGPT improves prompting without making experienced users feel constrained.
