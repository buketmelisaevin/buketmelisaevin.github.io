TFI Live Redesign

Adding depth without losing the simplicity users already trusted.

A research-led redesign exploring how TFI Live could improve route visualisation, real-time reassurance and journey planning while preserving familiar interactions.

Try the Prototype ↓ Watch Walkthrough
Role UX Researcher & Product Designer
Research Interviews, Think-Aloud & Usability Testing
Tools Figma

The Context

Simple worked.
But simple wasn't enough.

Initial research showed that users valued TFI Live because familiar journeys were easy to understand and required very little effort.

The limitation appeared when users needed more depth: understanding an unfamiliar route, comparing journeys visually, checking live transport information or moving from information to action.

The goal was therefore not to replace the existing experience. It was to extend what already worked.

Initial UX Audit

Where simplicity started to become restrictive

Straightforward information retrieval worked well, but the experience offered less support once the journey became unfamiliar or required more visual context.

01

Limited visual context

Journey results relied heavily on textual information, giving unfamiliar routes little geographic context.

02

Context switching

Users could depend on other mapping or tracking tools when the app did not provide enough information itself.

03

Information before action

The app worked well as an informational tool, but provided less support for acting on that information within the same flow.

User Research

First understand what shouldn't change.

Before deciding what to redesign, I explored which parts of the existing experience users already valued and where additional functionality would actually help.

2 Participants Moderated user interviews
Remote Research sessions
Think-Aloud During journey-planning tasks
Semi-Structured Interview format

What participants did

Participants completed journey-planning tasks while thinking aloud, allowing behaviour and moments of uncertainty to be observed alongside their verbal feedback.

What I investigated

I focused on task completion, feature clarity, confidence, pain points, expectations and moments where the current interface did not provide enough support.

How bias was reduced

Questions were framed neutrally, while follow-up prompts were used to explore participant reasoning without directing them toward a preferred answer.

i
Research limitation

This was an exploratory study with a small, relatively tech-comfortable sample. The findings were useful for directing the redesign, but broader testing would be needed before treating them as representative of the wider TFI user base.

Key Insights

Users wanted more depth, not more complexity.

01

Simplicity builds confidence

Familiar, low-effort flows helped users immediately understand their next step and reduced feelings of overwhelm.

02

Real-time information reassures

Current transport information created reassurance, particularly when users were uncertain about bus timing or reliability.

03

Simplicity can become restrictive

Minimalism became limiting when users needed richer route information, comparison or visual context.

04

Information needs a path to action

Friction increased when users obtained the information they needed but then had to change context to act on it.

Design Challenge

How can TFI Live give users more useful journey information without making the app harder to use?

Redesign Goals

Improve the experience without disrupting it.

01

Reduce cognitive effort

Reduce unnecessary switching, scrolling and repeated checking.

02

Improve visual understanding

Provide stronger visual context for routes, stops and unfamiliar journeys.

03

Reinforce trust

Make system status and real-time transport information easier to interpret.

04

Preserve familiar behaviour

Enhance established flows rather than changing interactions simply for novelty.

Key Redesign Decisions

Turning research into product decisions

01

INTEGRATED MAP VIEW

Keep visual and textual information together

The redesigned journey results combine route information with an interactive map, allowing users to understand geography and compare options without leaving the screen.

Less context switching Visual comparison Familiar map interaction
02

COMPACT ROUTE RESULTS

Show more useful information with less scrolling

Route results were condensed so key journey information could be scanned quickly while progressive disclosure preserved access to additional routes and detail.

Progressive disclosure Reduced scrolling Faster comparison
03

ICONOGRAPHY & COLOUR

Improve recognition and visual hierarchy

Familiar iconography, clearer visual hierarchy and more deliberate colour coding were used to improve recognition and make important actions easier to locate.

Recognition over recall Thumb-zone awareness Stronger hierarchy
04

LIVE TRACKING & ALERTS

Use real-time information to reduce uncertainty

Live status, bus position and alerts were surfaced contextually so users could receive reassurance without permanently increasing interface complexity.

System status Contextual controls Real-time reassurance

Usability Testing

Testing showed where the redesign still needed refinement.

The prototype improved visual understanding and reassurance, but testing also exposed important affordance and expectation issues.

KEY FRICTION

1:04
34 taps

Favourites discoverability

The participant spent over a minute trying to locate the Favourites action, indicating that its visual weight and affordance were not sufficiently clear.

Next iteration Increase visual weight and add a clear text label.

EXPECTATION GAP

!

Live tracking availability

Live tracking created reassurance, but it also created an expectation that every service would support it.

Next iteration Add an explicit “Live tracking unavailable” state when tracking is unsupported.

EXPLORATION

Journey detail depth

Additional route details could support exploration and decision-making without forcing users to leave the journey flow.

Next iteration Extend journey information while preserving progressive disclosure.

Reflection

Being clear was more powerful than being clever.

This project reinforced that redesign does not have to mean replacing familiar behaviour. Some of the strongest design opportunities came from understanding why users already trusted the existing experience.

The redesign added visual context, flexibility and real-time reassurance while retaining familiar interaction patterns. Testing then demonstrated how even a small affordance problem could create significant friction.

With more time, I would expand the sample beyond the initial exploratory users and validate the revised Favourites and live-tracking states across a broader range of transport users.
