Designing a clearer healthcare experience.

A redesign of the Vhi mobile app focused on improving service discovery, homepage hierarchy and navigation clarity while keeping the experience familiar.

The Context

Clearer structure for a growing ecosystem.

Vhi provides members with access to healthcare, insurance, support, wellbeing and partner services through one mobile application.

As that ecosystem grows, simply adding more destinations can make the experience harder to understand. Important functions can become buried inside categories while permanent navigation space becomes increasingly valuable.

I approached the redesign by looking first at the structure underneath the interface: navigation priorities, service discovery, homepage hierarchy and scalability.

Initial UX Audit

Users had to understand the app before they could find what they needed.

I reviewed the existing information hierarchy, bottom navigation and service-discovery paths to identify where the interface added unnecessary effort.

01

Navigation priorities

Permanent navigation space did not always reflect the tasks most members were likely to need.

Primary healthcare actions competed with lower-priority functions.
02

Service discoverability

Important services could sit inside broad categories that were difficult to predict without already knowing the app's structure.

Finding a service required category knowledge.
03

Homepage hierarchy

Core tasks and promotional content did not always have a strong enough distinction in the homepage hierarchy.

Useful actions needed greater prominence.
04

Scalability

As new services and partnerships are introduced, adding more permanent destinations would make the structure increasingly difficult to scan.

The architecture needed room to grow.
i
Project scope

This was an independent UX redesign based on interface evaluation and information architecture analysis. The solutions shown here are design hypotheses rather than findings from Vhi user research.

Design Challenge

How can Vhi make a growing range of services easier to find without overwhelming members or making the app feel unfamiliar?

01 • Navigation & Discoverability

Improve the routes before adding more features.

The first redesign decisions focused on giving members clearer ways to reach important functions without requiring them to understand Vhi's internal category structure.

01
BOTTOM NAVIGATION

Prioritise user goals.

Problem

Home was positioned at the edge, Parkrun occupied permanent navigation space and other important functions relied on broad categories or the More menu.

Current Design
↓
Redesign
Redesign rationale

Home moves to the centre, Claims becomes a permanent destination, Benefits consolidates promotional content and More remains available for secondary or future services. High-priority destinations are kept within the lower thumb zone, making common actions easier to reach during one-handed use.

02
UNIVERSAL SEARCH

Let users search by intent.

Problem

Without search, users needed to identify the correct category before they could locate a specific service.

Redesign
Redesign rationale

Universal search provides an alternative to category navigation. Members can search using familiar terms rather than remembering where a service sits inside the app.

02 • Homepage Experience

Put useful healthcare actions before promotional content.

The redesigned homepage creates a clearer sequence from orientation to action: familiar context, search, essential services, customisable shortcuts and then recommended content.

01

Familiarity, trust & orientation

A time-based greeting makes the experience feel responsive and familiar while confirming that the member is in their personalised Vhi environment before choosing what to do next.

02

Search stays visible

Universal search sits directly beneath the greeting instead of requiring members to browse through categories first.

03

Core services first

My Policy, Claims, Medical and Wellness provide immediate access to important high-level member needs.

04

Recommendations move down

Recommended features remain available, but they no longer compete with primary healthcare actions at the top of the screen.

03 • Personalisation

Give members control over what stays immediately available.

Different members use different services. Quick Actions allows frequently used services to remain visible without forcing every option into permanent navigation.

The interaction keeps the existing Vhi card language familiar while allowing the homepage to adapt to individual needs.

04 • Health Information Architecture

Organise services around what members are trying to do.

The Health area groups related services into predictable sections so the catalogue can grow without becoming one long undifferentiated list.

01

Get Care

Immediate healthcare access including Online Doctor, Urgent Care Clinics, Paediatric Clinics and Dermatology.

02

Health Support

Ongoing support such as Nurseline, Midwife Support, Online Physio and specialist services.

03

Health Tools

Supporting tools such as Symptom Checker and Health Check have their own predictable category.

04

Design Rationale

Improve access without redesigning familiarity out of the product.

01

Prioritise

Permanent space is reserved for tasks and destinations with greater member value.

02

Reduce recall

Search and predictable categories reduce the need to remember where a particular service lives.

03

Add user control

Customisable Quick Actions allow the homepage to better reflect individual member needs.

04

Design for growth

New services and partnerships can fit into the architecture without continuously expanding the main navigation.

Final Direction

One system. Clearer routes.

Navigation, search, customisation and service organisation work together as one information architecture rather than as isolated screen changes.

Reflection

Product redesign is often about deciding what deserves attention.

The most important part of this redesign was not creating additional features. It was reconsidering how existing and future services should be prioritised, grouped and accessed.

Moving Home to the centre, introducing search and reorganising healthcare services all reduce the amount of structural knowledge required from the member.

Customisable Quick Actions add another layer of flexibility by allowing different members to prioritise different services without increasing navigation complexity.

The project reinforced the value of preserving familiar interaction patterns while improving the information architecture underneath them.
